How the score works. Score = NRF9.3 nutrient density per 100 kcal (sum of %DV for protein, fiber, vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron, potassium and magnesium, each capped at 100%, minus %DV for saturated fat, added sugars (total sugars used as a proxy) and sodium). Per-100 kcal scoring favours foods that deliver nutrients for few calories; a food's absolute amounts per serving are in the table. Items are from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (2018) and reflect the products USDA analysed; current retail formulations may differ. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

What is the healthiest vegetable?

By nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3) Spinach, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt) ranks first of 60 vegetables in the USDA database, with 41 calories per 1 cup. Chard, swiss (cooked, boiled, drained, without salt) and Watercress, raw follow. The score rewards protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals and penalises saturated fat, sugar and sodium.

How are these vegetables ranked?

Vegetables ranked by nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3). Dark leafy greens dominate: they deliver vitamins A, C and K, calcium, iron, magnesium and potassium for almost no calories. Every value comes from USDA FoodData Central; see the methodology page for the formula.

Which vegetable should I avoid?

Tomatoes, yellow (raw) ranks last of 60 on this measure, with 21 calories per 1 cup, chopped. 'Last' here means lowest nutrient density per calorie, not that a food is harmful.
