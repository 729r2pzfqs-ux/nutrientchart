How the score works. Score = NRF9.3 nutrient density per 100 kcal (sum of %DV for protein, fiber, vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron, potassium and magnesium, each capped at 100%, minus %DV for saturated fat, added sugars (total sugars used as a proxy) and sodium). Per-100 kcal scoring favours foods that deliver nutrients for few calories; a food's absolute amounts per serving are in the table. Items are from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (2018) and reflect the products USDA analysed; current retail formulations may differ. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

What is the healthiest fruit?

By nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3) Limes, raw ranks first of 53 fruits in the USDA database, with 20 calories per 1 fruit (2" dia). Lemons, raw (without peel) and Oranges, raw (California, valencias) follow. The score rewards protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals and penalises saturated fat, sugar and sodium.

How are these fruits ranked?

Fresh fruits ranked by nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3). Berries, citrus and melons score highest because they pack vitamin C, potassium and fiber into few calories; dense sweet fruits like dates and bananas rank lower per calorie but remain good potassium sources. Every value comes from USDA FoodData Central; see the methodology page for the formula.

Which fruit should I avoid?

Dates, medjool ranks last of 53 on this measure, with 66 calories per 1 date, pitted. 'Last' here means lowest nutrient density per calorie, not that a food is harmful.
