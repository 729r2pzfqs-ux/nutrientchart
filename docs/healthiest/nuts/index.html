How the score works. Score = NRF9.3 nutrient density per 100 kcal (sum of %DV for protein, fiber, vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron, potassium and magnesium, each capped at 100%, minus %DV for saturated fat, added sugars (total sugars used as a proxy) and sodium). Per-100 kcal scoring favours foods that deliver nutrients for few calories; a food's absolute amounts per serving are in the table. Items are from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (2018) and reflect the products USDA analysed; current retail formulations may differ. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

What is the healthiest nuts and seeds?

By nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3) Seeds, sunflower seed kernels (dried) ranks first of 26 nuts and seeds in the USDA database, with 166 calories per 1 oz. Seeds, chia seeds (dried) and Nuts, almonds follow. The score rewards protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals and penalises saturated fat, sugar and sodium.

How are these nuts and seeds ranked?

Nuts and seeds ranked per ounce by nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3). All are calorie-dense, so the ranking rewards those with the most protein, fiber, magnesium, iron and vitamin E per calorie: pumpkin seeds, chia, flax, sunflower seeds and almonds. Every value comes from USDA FoodData Central; see the methodology page for the formula.

Which nuts and seeds should I avoid?

Nuts, coconut meat (dried (desiccated), toasted) ranks last of 26 on this measure, with 168 calories per 1 oz. 'Last' here means lowest nutrient density per calorie, not that a food is harmful.
