How the score works. Score = NRF9.3 nutrient density per 100 kcal (sum of %DV for protein, fiber, vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron, potassium and magnesium, each capped at 100%, minus %DV for saturated fat, added sugars (total sugars used as a proxy) and sodium), minus 10% of the %DV per 100 kcal for sodium as a category-specific tiebreaker. Per-100 kcal scoring favours foods that deliver nutrients for few calories; a food's absolute amounts per serving are in the table. Items are from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (2018) and reflect the products USDA analysed; current retail formulations may differ. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

What is the healthiest bread?

By nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3) Bread, white wheat ranks first of 30 breads in the USDA database, with 67 calories per 1 slice. Bread, reduced-calorie (wheat) and Bread, reduced-calorie (oat bran) follow. The score rewards protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals and penalises saturated fat, sugar and sodium.

How are these breads ranked?

Breads ranked by nutrient density per 100 calories using the NRF9.3 score, with sodium as an extra tiebreaker. Whole-grain and sprouted breads lead because they keep the fiber, magnesium and B vitamins that refining removes. Every value comes from USDA FoodData Central; see the methodology page for the formula.

Which bread should I avoid?

Bread, gluten-free (white, made with potato extract, rice starch, and rice flour) ranks last of 30 on this measure, with 109 calories per 1 slice. 'Last' here means lowest nutrient density per calorie, not that a food is harmful.
