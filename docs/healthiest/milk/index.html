How the score works. Score = NRF9.3 nutrient density per 100 kcal (sum of %DV for protein, fiber, vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron, potassium and magnesium, each capped at 100%, minus %DV for saturated fat, added sugars (total sugars used as a proxy) and sodium), minus 10% of the %DV per 100 kcal for sugars and saturated fat as a category-specific tiebreaker. Per-100 kcal scoring favours foods that deliver nutrients for few calories; a food's absolute amounts per serving are in the table. Items are from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (2018) and reflect the products USDA analysed; current retail formulations may differ. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

What is the healthiest milk?

By nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3) Beverages, almond milk (unsweetened, shelf stable) ranks first of 12 milks in the USDA database, with 39 calories per 1 cup. Soymilk, original and vanilla (light, with added calcium, vitamins A and D) and Soymilk, original and vanilla (light, unsweetened, with added calcium, vitamins A and D) follow. The score rewards protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals and penalises saturated fat, sugar and sodium.

How are these milks ranked?

Dairy and plant milks ranked per cup by nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3), with sugar and saturated fat as tiebreakers. Skim and low-fat dairy and fortified soy milk lead on protein and calcium per calorie; unsweetened almond milk is lowest in calories but also in protein. Every value comes from USDA FoodData Central; see the methodology page for the formula.

Which milk should I avoid?

Milk, whole (3.25% milkfat, with added vitamin D) ranks last of 12 on this measure, with 149 calories per 1 cup. 'Last' here means lowest nutrient density per calorie, not that a food is harmful.
