How the score works. Score = NRF9.3 nutrient density per 100 kcal (sum of %DV for protein, fiber, vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron, potassium and magnesium, each capped at 100%, minus %DV for saturated fat, added sugars (total sugars used as a proxy) and sodium), minus 10% of the %DV per 100 kcal for sodium and saturated fat as a category-specific tiebreaker. Per-100 kcal scoring favours foods that deliver nutrients for few calories; a food's absolute amounts per serving are in the table. Items are from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (2018) and reflect the products USDA analysed; current retail formulations may differ. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

What is the healthiest cheese?

By nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3) Cheese, mozzarella (part skim milk) ranks first of 33 cheeses in the USDA database, with 72 calories per 1 oz. Cheese, parmesan (hard) and Cheese, swiss follow. The score rewards protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals and penalises saturated fat, sugar and sodium.

How are these cheeses ranked?

Cheeses ranked per ounce by nutrient density (NRF9.3 per 100 calories), with sodium and saturated fat as tiebreakers. Fresh and soft cheeses score well on calories; hard aged cheeses deliver the most protein and calcium per ounce. Every value comes from USDA FoodData Central; see the methodology page for the formula.

Which cheese should I avoid?

Cheese, cream ranks last of 33 on this measure, with 99 calories per 1 oz. 'Last' here means lowest nutrient density per calorie, not that a food is harmful.
