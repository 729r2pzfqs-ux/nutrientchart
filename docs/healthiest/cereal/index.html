How the score works. Score = NRF9.3 nutrient density per 100 kcal (sum of %DV for protein, fiber, vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron, potassium and magnesium, each capped at 100%, minus %DV for saturated fat, added sugars (total sugars used as a proxy) and sodium), minus 10% of the %DV per 100 kcal for sugars as a category-specific tiebreaker. Per-100 kcal scoring favours foods that deliver nutrients for few calories; a food's absolute amounts per serving are in the table. Items are from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (2018) and reflect the products USDA analysed; current retail formulations may differ. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

What is the healthiest breakfast cereal?

By nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3) Cereals ready-to-eat, RALSTON Enriched Wheat Bran flakes ranks first of 60 cereals in the USDA database, with 113 calories per 1 serving (NLEA serving size = 0.75 cup). Cereals ready-to-eat, RALSTON TASTEEOS and Cereals ready-to-eat, GENERAL MILLS (CHEERIOS) follow. The score rewards protein, fiber, vitamins and minerals and penalises saturated fat, sugar and sodium.

How are these cereals ranked?

Ready-to-eat cereals ranked by nutrient density per 100 calories (NRF9.3), with sugar as the tiebreaker. Fortification adds vitamins and iron to many cereals, so high fiber and low sugar separate the genuinely healthy ones. Every value comes from USDA FoodData Central; see the methodology page for the formula.

Which breakfast cereal should I avoid?

Cereals ready-to-eat, HEALTH VALLEY (FIBER 7 Flakes) ranks last of 60 on this measure, with 109 calories per 0.75 cup. 'Last' here means lowest nutrient density per calorie, not that a food is harmful.
