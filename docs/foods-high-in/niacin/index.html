Foods high in niacin (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by niacin per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Fish, tuna leads with 18.8 mg per 3 oz (117% of the Daily Value), followed by Beef (14.9 mg) and Chicken (11.7 mg).

Niacin (vitamin B3) is used by hundreds of enzymes in energy metabolism and DNA repair. The Daily Value is 16 mg NE. Poultry, fish, meat, peanuts and whole grains are the richest sources.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for niacin is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in niacin?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Fish, tuna (yellowfin, fresh, cooked, dry heat) has the most niacin per serving: 18.8 mg per 3 oz (117% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 22.1 mg.

Which fruit has the most niacin?

Avocados, raw (all commercial varieties), with 3.5 mg per 1 avocado, NS as to Florida or California (22% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in niacin below.

Which vegetable has the most niacin?

Mushrooms, portabella (exposed to ultraviolet light, grilled), with 7.6 mg per 1 cup sliced (47% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in niacin below.

How much niacin do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 16 mg of niacin for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in niacin?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 79 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
