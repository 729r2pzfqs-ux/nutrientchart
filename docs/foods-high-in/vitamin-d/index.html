Foods high in vitamin D (per serving)

72 whole foods ranked by vitamin D per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Mushrooms, brown leads with 27.75 µg per 1 cup whole (139% of the Daily Value), followed by Mushrooms, portabella (24.42 µg) and Mushroom, white (18.34 µg).

Vitamin D is needed to absorb calcium and for bone, muscle and immune health. The Daily Value is 20 µg (800 IU). Few foods contain much: fatty fish, fish liver oil, egg yolks, UV-exposed mushrooms and fortified milk and cereal.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). The pool is 1,677 whole foods: fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, grains, fish, meat, poultry, dairy and eggs. Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for vitamin D is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in vitamin D?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Mushrooms, brown (italian, or crimini, exposed to ultraviolet light, raw) has the most vitamin D per serving: 27.75 µg per 1 cup whole (139% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 31.9 µg.

Which vegetable has the most vitamin D?

Mushrooms, brown (italian, or crimini, exposed to ultraviolet light, raw), with 27.75 µg per 1 cup whole (139% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in vitamin D below.

How much vitamin D do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 20 µg of vitamin D for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in vitamin D?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 10 of the 72 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
