Foods high in thiamin (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by thiamin per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Pork leads with 0.95 mg per 3 oz (79% of the Daily Value), followed by Pork, shoulder (0.62 mg) and Pigeonpeas, immature seeds (0.62 mg).

Thiamin (vitamin B1) helps convert carbohydrate into energy and supports nerve function. The Daily Value is 1.2 mg. Pork, fish, legumes, seeds and whole or enriched grains are the main sources.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for thiamin is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in thiamin?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Pork, fresh (shoulder, blade, boston (roasts), separable lean only, cooked, roasted) has the most thiamin per serving: 0.95 mg per 3 oz (79% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 1.12 mg.

Which fruit has the most thiamin?

Pomegranates, raw, with 0.19 mg per 1 pomegranate (4" dia) (16% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in thiamin below.

Which vegetable has the most thiamin?

Pigeonpeas, immature seeds (raw), with 0.62 mg per 1 cup (51% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in thiamin below.

How much thiamin do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 1.2 mg of thiamin for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in thiamin?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 46 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
