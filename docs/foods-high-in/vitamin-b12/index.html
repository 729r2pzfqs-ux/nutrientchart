Foods high in vitamin B12 (per serving)

91 whole foods ranked by vitamin B12 per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Mollusks, clam leads with 84.06 µg per 3 oz (3,502% of the Daily Value), followed by Beef (70.66 µg) and Mollusks, oyster (24.48 µg).

Vitamin B12 is needed for red blood cells, nerve function and DNA synthesis. The Daily Value is 2.4 µg. It occurs naturally only in animal foods (shellfish, fish, meat, eggs, dairy); vegans need fortified foods or supplements.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in vitamin B12?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Mollusks, clam (mixed species, cooked, moist heat) has the most vitamin B12 per serving: 84.06 µg per 3 oz (3,502% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 98.89 µg.

How much vitamin B12 do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 2.4 µg of vitamin B12 for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in vitamin B12?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 69 of the 91 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
