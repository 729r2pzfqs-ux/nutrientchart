Foods high in riboflavin (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by riboflavin per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Beef leads with 2.91 mg per 3 oz (224% of the Daily Value), followed by Turkey, all classes (2.28 mg) and Beef, plate steak (0.73 mg).

Riboflavin (vitamin B2) is needed for energy production and the metabolism of fats, drugs and steroids. The Daily Value is 1.3 mg. Dairy, eggs, organ meats, lean meat, almonds and mushrooms are good sources.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in riboflavin?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Beef, variety meats and by-products (liver, cooked, pan-fried) has the most riboflavin per serving: 2.91 mg per 3 oz (224% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 3.42 mg.

Which fruit has the most riboflavin?

Avocados, raw (all commercial varieties), with 0.26 mg per 1 avocado, NS as to Florida or California (20% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in riboflavin below.

Which vegetable has the most riboflavin?

Mushrooms, portabella (grilled), with 0.56 mg per 1 cup sliced (43% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in riboflavin below.

How much riboflavin do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 1.3 mg of riboflavin for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in riboflavin?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 67 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
