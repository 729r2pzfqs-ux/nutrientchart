Foods high in fiber (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by fiber per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Beans, navy leads with 19.1 g per 1 cup (68% of the Daily Value), followed by Beans, small white (18.6 g) and Beans, yellow (18.4 g).

Dietary fiber is the part of plant foods that is not digested. It supports regular digestion, feeds gut bacteria and helps moderate blood sugar and cholesterol. The Daily Value is 28 g; most people get about half that.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in fiber?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Beans, navy (mature seeds, cooked, boiled, without salt) has the most fiber per serving: 19.1 g per 1 cup (68% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 10.5 g.

Which fruit has the most fiber?

Avocados, raw (Florida), with 17 g per 1 fruit without skin and seeds (61% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in fiber below.

Which vegetable has the most fiber?

Edamame, frozen (prepared), with 9.7 g per 1 cup (34% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in fiber below.

How much fiber do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 28 g of fiber for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in fiber?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 44 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
