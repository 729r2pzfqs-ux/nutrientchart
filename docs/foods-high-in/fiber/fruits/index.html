How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). The pool is 1,677 whole foods: fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, grains, fish, meat, poultry, dairy and eggs. Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for fiber is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in fiber?

Among fruits in the USDA database, Avocados, raw (Florida) has the most fiber per serving: 17 g per 1 fruit without skin and seeds (61% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 5.6 g.

How much fiber do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 28 g of fiber for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in fiber?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 7 of the 50 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
