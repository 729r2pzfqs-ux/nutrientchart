Foods high in vitamin E (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by vitamin E per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Seeds, sunflower seed kernels leads with 10.3 mg per 1 oz (69% of the Daily Value), followed by Avocados, raw (8.09 mg) and Nuts, almonds (7.36 mg).

Vitamin E (alpha-tocopherol) is a fat-soluble antioxidant that protects cell membranes. The Daily Value is 15 mg. Sunflower seeds, almonds, hazelnuts, vegetable oils and leafy greens are the main sources.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in vitamin E?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Seeds, sunflower seed kernels (oil roasted, without salt) has the most vitamin E per serving: 10.3 mg per 1 oz (69% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 36.33 mg.

Which fruit has the most vitamin E?

Avocados, raw (Florida), with 8.09 mg per 1 fruit without skin and seeds (54% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in vitamin E below.

Which vegetable has the most vitamin E?

Spinach, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt), with 3.74 mg per 1 cup (25% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in vitamin E below.

How much vitamin E do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 15 mg of vitamin E for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in vitamin E?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 14 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
