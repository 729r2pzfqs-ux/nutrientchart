Foods high in caffeine (per serving)

12 whole foods ranked by caffeine per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Beverages, coffee leads with 95 mg per 1 cup (8 fl oz), followed by Beverages, Energy Drink with carbonated water and high fructose corn syrup (91 mg) and Beverages, Energy Drink (79 mg).

Caffeine is a stimulant found in coffee, tea, cocoa and energy drinks. There is no Daily Value; the FDA cites 400 mg a day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects in healthy adults. Values are milligrams per serving.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). The pool is 1,677 whole foods: fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, grains, fish, meat, poultry, dairy and eggs. Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for caffeine is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in caffeine?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Beverages, coffee (brewed, prepared with tap water) has the most caffeine per serving: 95 mg per 1 cup (8 fl oz). Per 100 g it contains 40 mg.

Is there a Daily Value for caffeine?

No. The FDA has not set a Daily Value for caffeine, so this list shows mg per serving without a percentage. Caffeine is a stimulant found in coffee, tea, cocoa and energy drinks. There is no Daily Value; the FDA cites 400 mg a day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects in healthy adults. Values are milligrams per serving.
