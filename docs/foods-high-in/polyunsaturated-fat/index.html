Foods high in polyunsaturated fat (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by polyunsaturated fat per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Nuts, walnuts leads with 14 g per 1 oz (14 halves), followed by Seeds, hemp seed (10.8 g) and Seeds, sunflower seed kernels (10.6 g).

Polyunsaturated fats include the essential omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids the body cannot make. Seeds, nuts, vegetable oils and fatty fish are the richest sources. There is no Daily Value; values here are grams per serving.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). The pool is 1,677 whole foods: fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, grains, fish, meat, poultry, dairy and eggs. Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for polyunsaturated fat is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in polyunsaturated fat?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Nuts, walnuts (english) has the most polyunsaturated fat per serving: 14 g per 1 oz (14 halves). Per 100 g it contains 49.3 g.

Which fruit has the most polyunsaturated fat?

Avocados, raw (Florida), with 5.1 g per 1 fruit without skin and seeds. See the full list of fruits high in polyunsaturated fat below.

Which vegetable has the most polyunsaturated fat?

Peppers, sweet (green, sauteed), with 6.8 g per 1 cup chopped. See the full list of vegetables high in polyunsaturated fat below.

Is there a Daily Value for polyunsaturated fat?

No. The FDA has not set a Daily Value for polyunsaturated fat, so this list shows g per serving without a percentage.
