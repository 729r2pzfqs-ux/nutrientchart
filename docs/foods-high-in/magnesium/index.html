Foods high in magnesium (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by magnesium per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Seeds, hemp seed leads with 198 mg per 1 oz (47% of the Daily Value), followed by Spinach, cooked (157 mg) and Seeds, pumpkin and squash seed kernels (156 mg).

Magnesium takes part in more than 300 enzyme reactions, including energy production, muscle and nerve function and blood pressure regulation. The Daily Value is 420 mg. Seeds, nuts, legumes, whole grains and leafy greens are the richest sources.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in magnesium?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Seeds, hemp seed (hulled) has the most magnesium per serving: 198 mg per 1 oz (47% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 700 mg.

Which fruit has the most magnesium?

Horned melon (Kiwano), with 84 mg per 1 fruit (4-2/3" long x 2-3/4" dia) (20% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in magnesium below.

Which vegetable has the most magnesium?

Spinach, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt), with 157 mg per 1 cup (37% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in magnesium below.

How much magnesium do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 420 mg of magnesium for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in magnesium?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 36 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
