Foods high in omega-3 (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by omega-3 per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Seeds, flaxseed leads with 5.51 g per 1 oz, followed by Seeds, chia seeds (5.05 g) and Nuts, walnuts (2.57 g).

Omega-3 fatty acids: ALA from plant foods (flax, chia, walnuts) and EPA and DHA from fish and seafood. The Adequate Intake for ALA is 1.6 g a day for men and 1.1 g for women; there is no FDA Daily Value, so no %DV is shown.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). The pool is 1,677 whole foods: fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, grains, fish, meat, poultry, dairy and eggs. Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for omega-3 is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in omega-3?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Seeds, flaxseed has the most omega-3 per serving: 5.51 g per 1 oz. Per 100 g it contains 19.42 g.

Which fruit has the most omega-3?
