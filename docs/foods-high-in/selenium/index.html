Foods high in selenium (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by selenium per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Nuts, brazilnuts leads with 543 µg per 1 oz (6 kernels) (988% of the Daily Value), followed by Mollusks, oyster (131 µg) and Fish, tuna (92 µg).

Selenium is part of antioxidant enzymes and is needed for thyroid hormone metabolism. The Daily Value is 55 µg. Brazil nuts, seafood, meat, eggs and grains are the main sources; one Brazil nut can exceed the DV.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for selenium is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in selenium?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Nuts, brazilnuts (dried, unblanched) has the most selenium per serving: 543 µg per 1 oz (6 kernels) (988% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 1,917 µg.

Which fruit has the most selenium?

Peaches, yellow (raw), with 3.1 µg per 1 medium (2-2/3" dia) (5.7% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in selenium below.

Which vegetable has the most selenium?

Mushrooms, shiitake (cooked, without salt), with 36 µg per 1 cup pieces (65% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in selenium below.

How much selenium do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 55 µg of selenium for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in selenium?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 91 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
