Foods high in vitamin A (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by vitamin A per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Turkey, all classes leads with 9,138 µg per 3 oz (1,015% of the Daily Value), followed by Beef (8,026 µg) and Sweet potato, cooked (2,581 µg).

Vitamin A supports vision, immune function and cell growth. The Daily Value is 900 µg RAE (retinol activity equivalents), which counts both preformed vitamin A from animal foods and carotenoids from orange and dark-green vegetables.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in vitamin A?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Turkey, all classes (liver, cooked, simmered) has the most vitamin A per serving: 9,138 µg per 3 oz (1,015% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 10,751 µg.

Which fruit has the most vitamin A?

Mangos, raw, with 181 µg per 1 fruit without refuse (20% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in vitamin A below.

Which vegetable has the most vitamin A?

Sweet potato, cooked (boiled, without skin), with 2,581 µg per 1 cup, mashed (287% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in vitamin A below.

How much vitamin A do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 900 µg of vitamin A for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in vitamin A?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 26 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
