Foods high in zinc (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by zinc per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Mollusks, oyster leads with 77.3 mg per 3 oz (703% of the Daily Value), followed by Beef, chuck (10.4 mg) and Beef, ribeye filet (9.4 mg).

Zinc supports immune function, wound healing, taste and smell, and protein and DNA synthesis. The Daily Value is 11 mg. Oysters, red meat, poultry, beans, nuts and whole grains are good sources; zinc from plants is less well absorbed.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in zinc?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Mollusks, oyster (eastern, canned) has the most zinc per serving: 77.3 mg per 3 oz (703% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 91 mg.

Which fruit has the most zinc?

Avocados, raw (all commercial varieties), with 1.3 mg per 1 avocado, NS as to Florida or California (12% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in zinc below.

Which vegetable has the most zinc?

Edamame, frozen (prepared), with 2.2 mg per 1 cup (20% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in zinc below.

How much zinc do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 11 mg of zinc for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in zinc?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 62 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
