Foods high in protein (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by protein per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Tempeh leads with 33.7 g per 1 cup (67% of the Daily Value), followed by Soybeans (31.3 g) and Game meat, deer (30.8 g).

Protein supplies the amino acids used to build and repair muscle, skin, enzymes and hormones. The Daily Value is 50 g; needs rise with body weight, age and training.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). The pool is 1,677 whole foods: fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, grains, fish, meat, poultry, dairy and eggs. Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for protein is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in protein?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Tempeh has the most protein per serving: 33.7 g per 1 cup (67% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 20.3 g.

Which fruit has the most protein?

Avocados, raw (Florida), with 6.8 g per 1 fruit without skin and seeds (14% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in protein below.

Which vegetable has the most protein?

Soybeans, green (cooked, boiled, drained, without salt), with 22.2 g per 1 cup (44% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in protein below.

How much protein do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 50 g of protein for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in protein?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 98 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
