Foods high in copper (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by copper per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Beef leads with 12.4 mg per 3 oz (1,378% of the Daily Value), followed by Mollusks, oyster (4.85 mg) and Vermicelli, made from soy (2.68 mg).

Copper is needed for iron metabolism, energy production, connective tissue and the nervous system. The Daily Value is 0.9 mg. Shellfish, organ meats, nuts, seeds, chocolate and whole grains are rich sources.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for copper is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in copper?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Beef, variety meats and by-products (liver, cooked, pan-fried) has the most copper per serving: 12.4 mg per 3 oz (1,378% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 14.59 mg.

Which fruit has the most copper?

Avocados, raw (Florida), with 0.94 mg per 1 fruit without skin and seeds (105% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in copper below.

Which vegetable has the most copper?

Mushrooms, shiitake (cooked, without salt), with 1.3 mg per 1 cup pieces (144% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in copper below.

How much copper do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 0.9 mg of copper for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in copper?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 100 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
