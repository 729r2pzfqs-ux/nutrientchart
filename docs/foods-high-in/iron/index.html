Foods high in iron (per serving)

100 whole foods ranked by iron per common serving, from USDA FoodData Central. Soybeans leads with 8.8 mg per 1 cup (49% of the Daily Value), followed by Beans, white (7.8 mg) and Mollusks, oyster (7.8 mg).

Iron carries oxygen in haemoglobin. The Daily Value is 18 mg. Heme iron from meat, fish and poultry is absorbed far better than non-heme iron from plants; vitamin C eaten at the same meal improves plant-iron absorption.

How this list is built. Values are from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, with Foundation Foods analytical updates where available). The pool is 1,677 whole foods: fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts and seeds, grains, fish, meat, poultry, dairy and eggs. Spices, herbs, baby foods, powders, flours, dried and freeze-dried items, juices, processed meats, organ meats other than liver and uncommon foods are excluded; meats, fish, grains and legumes are ranked cooked. Each food family appears once and no single animal or plant fills more than six places. Ranking is by amount per serving; the per-100 g column is shown for reference. A food that USDA has not analysed for iron is left out rather than counted as zero. Full methodology.

Frequently asked questions

Which food is highest in iron?

Among whole foods in the USDA database, Soybeans, mature cooked (boiled, without salt) has the most iron per serving: 8.8 mg per 1 cup (49% of the Daily Value). Per 100 g it contains 5.1 mg.

Which fruit has the most iron?

Horned melon (Kiwano), with 2.4 mg per 1 fruit (4-2/3" long x 2-3/4" dia) (13% of the Daily Value). See the full list of fruits high in iron below.

Which vegetable has the most iron?

Spinach, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt), with 6.4 mg per 1 cup (36% of the Daily Value). See the full list of vegetables high in iron below.

How much iron do I need per day?

The FDA Daily Value used on Nutrition Facts labels is 18 mg of iron for adults and children aged 4 and over. Individual needs vary with age, sex, pregnancy and health conditions.

What counts as a food high in iron?

FDA labeling rules call a food a 'good source' when one serving provides 10 to 19% of the Daily Value and 'high' or 'excellent' at 20% or more. 32 of the 100 foods on this list reach 20% DV per serving.
