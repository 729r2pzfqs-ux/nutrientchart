Vitamin D in salmon

About vitamin D

Vitamin D is needed to absorb calcium and for bone, muscle and immune health. The Daily Value is 20 µg (800 IU). Few foods contain much: fatty fish, fish liver oil, egg yolks, UV-exposed mushrooms and fortified milk and cereal.

Foods with more vitamin D than salmon

  1. Mushrooms, brown (italian, or crimini, exposed to ultraviolet light, raw): 27.75 µg per 1 cup whole (139% DV)
  2. Mushrooms, portabella (exposed to ultraviolet light, raw): 24.42 µg per 1 cup diced (122% DV)
  3. Mushroom, white (exposed to ultraviolet light, raw): 18.34 µg per 1 cup pieces or slices (92% DV)
  4. Salmon, sockeye (canned, drained solids, without skin and bones): 18.27 µg per 3 oz (91% DV)
  5. Fish, salmon (sockeye, canned, drained solids): 17.85 µg per 3 oz (89% DV)

Frequently asked questions

How much vitamin D is in salmon?

Salmon has 11.13 µg of vitamin D per 3 oz (85 g), which is 56% of the Daily Value. Per 100 g it contains 13.1 µg.

Is salmon high in vitamin D?

Yes. At 56% of the Daily Value per serving, salmon counts as an excellent source under FDA labeling rules (10% is 'good', 20% is 'high').

How much vitamin D do I need a day?

The FDA Daily Value is 20 µg. One 3 oz of salmon covers 56% of it.
