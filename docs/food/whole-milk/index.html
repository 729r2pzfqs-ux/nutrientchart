Whole milk nutrition facts

Whole milk (1 cup, 244 g) has 149 calories, 8 g protein, 0 g fiber, 7.8 g fat. It is an excellent source of vitamin B12 (55% DV), riboflavin (26% DV), calcium (23% DV) and a good source of phosphorus and pantothenic acid.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories are in whole milk?

Whole milk has 149 calories per 1 cup (244 g), or 61 calories per 100 g, according to USDA FoodData Central.

How much protein is in whole milk?

8 g of protein per 1 cup, which is 16% of the 50 g Daily Value.

What is whole milk rich in?

Per serving, whole milk is richest in Vitamin B12 at 55% of the Daily Value, Riboflavin (B2) (26%), Calcium (23%), Phosphorus (20%).

Is whole milk healthy?

By nutrient density, whole milk scores 8 on the NRF9.3 index (nine nutrients to encourage minus three to limit, per 100 kcal). Its nutrient profile is below average compared with other whole foods. See the methodology page for how both scores work; this is data, not medical advice.
