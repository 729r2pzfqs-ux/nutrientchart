Vitamin K in kale

Kale has 81.9 µg of vitamin K per 1 cup (21 g), which is 68% of the Daily Value. Per 100 g it contains 390 µg.

About vitamin K

Vitamin K is needed for blood clotting and bone metabolism. The Daily Value is 120 µg. Leafy greens (kale, spinach, collards), broccoli and Brussels sprouts contain very large amounts. People on warfarin should keep intake consistent.

Foods with more vitamin K than kale

  1. Parsley, fresh: 984 µg per 1 cup chopped (820% DV)
  2. Spinach, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt): 888 µg per 1 cup (740% DV)
  3. Mustard greens, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt): 830 µg per 1 cup, chopped (692% DV)
  4. Collards, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt): 773 µg per 1 cup, chopped (644% DV)
  5. Beet greens, cooked (boiled, drained, without salt): 697 µg per 1 cup (1" pieces) (581% DV)

Frequently asked questions

How much vitamin K is in kale?

Kale has 81.9 µg of vitamin K per 1 cup (21 g), which is 68% of the Daily Value. Per 100 g it contains 390 µg.

Is kale high in vitamin K?

Yes. At 68% of the Daily Value per serving, kale counts as an excellent source under FDA labeling rules (10% is 'good', 20% is 'high').

How much vitamin K do I need a day?

The FDA Daily Value is 120 µg. One 1 cup of kale covers 68% of it.
